Electromobility in Mexico: EV Adoption in Last-Mile Logistics

Kira Darian1

1Tecnológico de Monterrey · School of Engineering and Sciences

RESEARCH PROJECTEV / MXLast-mile mobility

01 · Research question

Which operational and business characteristics are associated with current EV use and consideration of adoption among Mexican small-cargo operators?

Why this matters

Interest in electrification is not the same as having an electric vehicle in service. Distinguishing the two is essential for targeted fleet-transition strategies.

Scope: eligible small-cargo firms.

02 · Data & Methodology

Out of 105 total survey submissions (representing 92 distinct rows and 62 eligible operators), sample sizes varied across questions because missing responses were excluded on a per-question basis.

01

Audit

Remove exact duplicates, apply eligibility rules, flag ambiguous answers, preserve originals.

02

Description

Question-specific rates, perceived barriers, firm and fleet characteristics.

03

Model

Binary classifiers for EV use and EV consideration; stratified nested validation across three split seeds.

Insights are intended to inform business-model and policy recommendations.

03 · Adoption–consideration gap

25.0%currently use EVs
57.6%considered EVs
Consideration among non-users

Of the 44 responding operators not currently using EVs, 19 (43.2%) had considered them. This is a subgroup, not an estimated adoption-conversion rate.

04 · Perceived barriers & benefits

Perceived Advantages

Lower emissions (59.3%): Reduced tailpipe pollution and Operating-cost savings (50.8%): Potential reductions in energy and maintenance expenses.

Perceived Disadvantages

Charging infrastructure (52.5%): Limited availability, accessibility, and charging capacity and Driving range (45.8%): Concerns about route coverage, battery autonomy, and operational interruptions.

05 · Exploratory predictive evidence

Two binary targets were evaluated separately using fleet and operational characteristics. The models explore predictive signals; they do not identify causal drivers.

EV use

Random Forest with extended features: balanced accuracy ≈ 0.693 in the supplied poster-model summary.

EV consideration

Linear SVM, fleet baseline: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.681 in the supplied poster-model summary.

06 · Key findings & implications

  • Interest exceeds current adoption: 25.0% currently use EVs, while 57.6% report consideration. Among non-users, 43.2% have considered adoption.
  • Charging and range remain key concerns: 52.5% identified charging infrastructure and 45.8% driving range as perceived barriers.
  • Operational characteristics show predictive value: Random Forest achieved 0.726 balanced accuracy for current EV use, although model performance remains exploratory.

Conclusion. The findings suggest an opportunity for fleet electrification, but converting interest into adoption requires evaluating charging feasibility, route compatibility, and actual operating costs.
